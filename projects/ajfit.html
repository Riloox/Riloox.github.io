AJFit

A training app for a personal trainer and the people he coaches. Private repo, paying client.

Why it exists

A personal trainer asked me to build the app he wanted for his clients. The coaching part was already solved; what was missing was a place where a client logs a session, leaves a note about how it felt, and where he can see who is waiting for an answer without scrolling through chat.

So AJFit has two audiences and two different screens. Clients get the session log, progress per exercise, weekly volume per muscle group, and meal ideas built from the ingredients they say they have at home. The trainer gets one list, ordered by what needs attention: unanswered feedback first, then clients who stopped training.

What was actually hard

The coach screen was the real product. A dashboard of every client is easy to build and useless, because the trainer already knows his clients. What he does not know at 8am is which one replied last night and which one has not shown up in three weeks. Sorting by that, with a reason attached, took more iterations than the rest of the app.

Progress numbers were the second thing. A chart per exercise only means something if the same exercise is the same exercise every week, so the exercise catalog has to be strict about what counts as a variation, and the weekly volume has to be summed the same way every time. The first version was generous with free text and the charts lied politely.

The client side had its own constraint: it gets used mid-workout, on a phone, with one hand. That killed a couple of designs that looked fine on a laptop and slowed the logging down to the point where nobody would do it between sets.

Then there is the boring part that a client app cannot skip: each client only sees their own data, the invite flow has to be simple enough for a trainer to run without me, and the whole thing has to cost close to nothing to host, which is why it sits on Cloudflare Workers and D1.

What I'd change

I would build the coach attention list first and let the client screens grow around it. It is the feature that decided whether the app was worth opening, and I found that out later than I should have.

I would make the exercise catalog strict from day one, with free text only as a note. Migrating exercises after clients have logged months of sessions is the kind of cleanup that makes you question the original design.

The meal ideas would come last. They are the feature people ask for and the one that needs data clients will not keep updated, so it works best as a small suggestion on top of a good log, not as a reason to open the app.
