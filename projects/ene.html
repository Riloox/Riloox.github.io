Ene

A Spanish tutor for Korean speakers. A local 2B model fine-tuned from Gemma 4 E2B, plus a small demo app. Written Eñe in Spanish.

Why it exists

Learning Spanish as a Korean speaker has a specific problem: the questions you want to ask are about grammar, and the answers you find are written in English or in Spanish you cannot read yet. The version I wanted was simple. Write a sentence, ask in Korean if it sounds right, get the corrected sentence, a short note on what changed, the rule behind it in Korean, and one exercise.

Ene has two halves. The app is a short travel lesson with a mascot, voice input through the Web Speech API, invite codes and rate limits, running on a free Cloudflare Worker with KV. The tutor behind it is a fine-tune of Gemma 4 E2B, a 2B model, trained on one 12 GB laptop GPU and served locally through Ollama.

The demo has no public link. It sits behind an invite code, and I hand those out by hand. If you want to try it, email me and I will send you one, usually the same day.

What was actually hard

Training the model was the easy half. Measuring it was the hard one. On the first frozen set, 208 items split evenly between sentences with real errors and sentences that were already correct, the stock model scored 123. The fine-tuned v4 scored 177, which looks like a large jump until you look at where it came from: the stock model rewrote two out of three correct sentences, and most of the gain was it learning to leave them alone. On the error half it actually trailed the stock model, 81 against 90, p=0.064.

The rounds that failed are more useful than the one that worked. SFT taught the model the format quickly, and it started praising wrong sentences in that format. GRPO on top of v4 added nothing measurable, 179 against 177, p=0.75, after a night of training. The fix that did work was less clever: a rewrite-first prompt, teacher-written items for the cases the model kept getting wrong, and a test set frozen before training so the numbers could not drift with it.

Then the judge problem appeared. A 2B model can produce the right correction and the wrong explanation, and string matching will not catch it. I built a separate judge around a blind 26B model, which caught things like translating perros as street cats when confirming a sentence was correct, and calling pierdas an infinitive. When all three models were scored again on the later set, the mechanical 184 for the last round became 181 with the judge, 95% interval 81.8% to 90.9%. The honest reading is that the 184 was a little optimistic.

The rest was plumbing: QLoRA on 12 GB, merge, convert to GGUF, quantise to Q4_K_M, import into Ollama. llama.cpp's converter drops Gemma 4's vision and audio encoders, so this is a text tutor whether I like it or not.

What I'd change

I would build the frozen test set and the judge before the first training run, not after the fourth. Two of the rounds I spent were chasing a number that the evaluation setup could not measure properly, and the judge changed the ranking of the models when it arrived.

I would skip GRPO entirely for this task. It cost a night per round and moved the score by two items, which is noise. The reward was checking the verdict and the rewritten Spanish, so it could never fix the part that was actually wrong, which was the explanation.

And I would get a Korean speaker involved earlier. The explanations are in Korean, I do not speak Korean, and the blind judge is a proxy, not a replacement. There is a 30-item calibration set waiting for a native speaker to label it. Until that happens, the Korean quality is checked by a model, and I say so instead of pretending otherwise.
