The Riloox video pipeline

The editing and asset pipeline behind my YouTube channel: narrated videos about Linux, developer tools and games.

Why it exists

These are voice-only commentary videos: a script, a recorded voice-over, and a cut every three or four seconds over stock photos, screenshots and screen recordings. Editing one by hand in Kdenlive is slow and, worse, repetitive. The same decisions come back every time: where a pause goes, which photo matches the sentence, when a sound effect is allowed, how loud the music bed sits.

So the rules went into a document and the mechanical parts went into scripts. A build script takes the voice-over and a shot list, applies the pauses marked on it, and writes an MLT timeline that melt renders. Word timings come from the transcript, so cuts can land on the word instead of near it. The audio library is normalised to -16 LUFS before it is used, and the fetchers only pull assets that are CC0 or public domain.

What was actually hard

Time is the first problem. Inserting a pause at 42 seconds moves everything after it, and every cue in the shot list is written against the original recording. The build script keeps the shot list in original times and maps them forward through the pause list, which means one small function decides whether a sound effect lands on the punchline or half a second early. It is a dozen lines and it caused most of the bugs.

The second problem is that the interesting decisions are not automatable. Matching a photo to the intent of a sentence (sarcasm needs smug, not angry) is taste, and the pipeline only records the decision. What it can do is refuse to let me be lazy: every cue needs a real file path from the library, so a placeholder cannot survive to the render.

The third is licensing, which is a hard rule rather than a preference. Every asset has to be usable without attribution and without buying anything, so the fetchers pull from sources that allow exactly that and each download folder keeps a source file as proof. That rules out most of the internet, and browsing for material by hand is the slowest part of an edit.

The last one was a surprise, and it came from the analytics: the edit was not the bottleneck. The first videos got impressions and lost the audience in the first fifteen seconds, with a click-through rate around 1%. A better pipeline does not fix a thumbnail that promises something the opening does not deliver.

What I'd change

I would build the timeline generator before editing the second video by hand, not the fourth. The shot list format is the useful artifact: once a video is a table of lines, visuals and pauses, it can be reviewed, diffed and rebuilt.

I would transcribe first and write the shot list against the transcript from the beginning. Doing it the other way means re-timing cues after every re-record, which is exactly the work the pipeline was supposed to remove.

And I would look at retention before polishing anything else. The pipeline made the edits cheaper, but the first fifteen seconds decide whether anyone sees them, and that is a writing problem, not a rendering one.
